\begin{letter}{The Editors-in-Chief\\The VLDB Journal}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the manuscript \emph{``Not All Embeddings Are Gaussian: Revisiting Adaptive HNSW Search''}
by Pranav Shenvi, M G Shrivardhan Swaroop, Megha Shah (PES University), Kiran Hombal (University of
Illinois Urbana-Champaign) and Shylaja S S (PES University) for consideration as a research article in
The VLDB Journal.

The paper is an experimental study of per-query search breadth in graph-based nearest-neighbor
search. Ada-ef (SIGMOD 2026) chooses the search breadth (\emph{ef}) of each HNSW query from a score
that assumes a query's similarities to the database are Gaussian. We test that assumption query by
query on 41 public datasets and re-evaluate the method end to end on 20 of them. The baseline is a
hindsight-tuned fixed \emph{ef}, matched on both mean and worst-case recall. The main findings are:
\begin{itemize}
\item The Gaussian departs significantly on nine datasets, six of them learned image, audio and
  recommendation embeddings, and the data type does not predict which.
\item The departure hurts Ada-ef's ranking of queries less than its size suggests. The score
  collapses only on the most extreme datasets, and in controlled data where the hard queries move
  away from the bulk.
\item What limits Ada-ef in practice is its fixed per-query overhead: a 1025-distance probe and a
  $d \times d$ variance computation. It is slower than the tuned fixed \emph{ef} in 28 of 34 runs.
  A sweep of probe lengths for both methods on 17 datasets separates the two costs.
\item A nonparametric variant that reads its thresholds from the data (PercEF) ranks queries about as
  well at equal probe length, and is cheaper than the tuned fixed \emph{ef} in 32 of 34 runs.
\end{itemize}
The study also compares DARTH, LAET and Patience in Proximity. It checks robustness to target
recall, $k$, HNSW degree, random seed and metric. It reports seven pitfalls in evaluating adaptive
search, including one that affected our own first evaluation of Ada-ef, and ends with guidelines
for practitioners.

We believe the paper fits The VLDB Journal's interest in thorough experimental analyses of data
management techniques. Its length (18 pages in the journal's two-column template) allows the full
per-dataset results that a conference version would leave out. All code, run scripts and the result
files behind every table and figure are available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/PercEF/}; every number can be regenerated from them in minutes.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration for publication
elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests.
